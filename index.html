How does an EMI calculator work?

An EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is the fixed amount you pay every month until your loan is fully repaid. Each installment has two parts: a portion that reduces the loan you borrowed (the principal) and a portion that pays the interest or profit charged by the bank. In the early months most of your installment goes to interest, and over time more of it goes to principal.

The EMI formula (reducing balance)

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)
  • P = loan amount in PKR
  • r = monthly rate = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
  • n = number of monthly installments

Example

For a loan of Rs 1,000,000 at 15% per year for 5 years (60 months), the monthly installment comes to roughly Rs 23,790, and you repay about Rs 427,000 as interest over the full period. Try the same numbers in the calculator above to see the schedule.

Flat rate vs reducing balance

With a flat rate, interest is calculated on the full original amount for the entire tenure. With a reducing balance rate, interest is calculated only on what you still owe. The same quoted number costs you less on a reducing balance, so compare offers carefully and ask which method your bank uses.

Tips to lower your loan cost

  • Compare offers from several banks, including processing fees and insurance.
  • Choose the shortest tenure you can comfortably afford, since a longer tenure means more total interest.
  • Make a larger down payment to reduce the amount you borrow.
  • Ask your bank about early settlement or part-payment charges before you sign.

Frequently asked questions

What is an EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment. It is the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan, and it includes both a part of the principal and the interest or profit.

How is EMI calculated?

For a reducing balance loan, EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly installments.

What is the difference between flat rate and reducing balance rate?

With a flat rate, interest is charged on the full original loan amount for the whole tenure. With a reducing balance rate, interest is charged only on the amount still owed, so the same quoted rate costs less. Always ask your bank which method it uses.

Can I use this calculator for Islamic financing?

You can use it to estimate the monthly installment if you enter the profit rate quoted by your bank. Actual installments for products such as Diminishing Musharakah or Ijarah may be structured differently, so confirm the final figures with your bank.

Is the result exactly what my bank will charge?

No. This tool gives an estimate. Banks may add processing fees, insurance, taxes and rate changes, and may round figures differently. Always check the official offer from your bank.
